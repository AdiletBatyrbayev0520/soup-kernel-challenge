## Blackwell (sm_120) notes for the 5090 profile

- Consumer Blackwell has no wgmma/tcgen05/TMEM. Triton 3.9 lowers `tl.dot` to `mma.sync`, so
  datacenter-Blackwell (sm_100) GEMM tricks don't apply. It has 99 KB shared memory per block,
  170 SMs, 96 MB L2 and about 1.8 TB/s.
- Our kernels are memory-bound row kernels, so the lever is the launch config. The CE kernel
  sweep on bf16 [2048, 151936] picked BLOCK=4096 with 4 warps: 1,538 GB/s for stats and
  1,406 GB/s for grad, against 850 GB/s for the worst config. NF4 dequant uses BLOCK=2048:
  21.6 µs vs bnb's 28.6 µs on an 8960×1536 weight. Only the 5090 entry is measured. The
  t4/3090/4090 entries are extrapolated and need a re-sweep on those cards.
- Not done (they would change numerics or need more work): TF32 for the fp32 lm_head
  (changes the logits), and causal SDPA without the padding mask (flash path, about
  169 µs vs 254 µs+).

## Limits / known issues

- One run per side on a 5090 in bf16. The jury measures a T4 in fp16, 2 runs per side, and
  the ratios there will differ. Run `templates/kaggle_notebook.ipynb` on this branch before
  trusting them.
- On its own, `feature/nf4-dequant` is 2% slower end-to-end although its kernel is faster.
  The likely cause is Python/Triton launch overhead from about 400 extra launches per step.
  It is inferred, not profiled.
- The fused CE reads Soup's private `ChunkedStep` / `_log_trl_metrics`
  (`soup_cli.utils.stream_loss_chunk`). It turns on no Soup switch, but the jury may ask
  about it.
- The `solution/machines.py` entries for the t4, 3090 and 4090 are unmeasured.
